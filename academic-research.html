Research & Scholarly Writing

Research papers written during my MA TESOL studies at Brigham Young University.

A selection of research papers written during my MA TESOL studies at Brigham Young University — spanning applied data analysis projects, a literature review, and an original research proposal.

Data Analysis

Reading Assessment Project

  • Data Analysis
  • Item Analysis
  • Psychometrics

For a graduate course on language assessment, our team designed a standardized reading proficiency exam for students at English Language Center in Provo and analyzed the results using Jamovi. We examined score distributions and percent-score trends across proficiency levels, then evaluated individual exam items using item facility, point-biserial correlation, and upper-lower discrimination to judge how well the test and its questions performed.

Data Analysis

Methods for Measuring Writing Accuracy

  • GAR & EFCR Methods
  • Writing Assessment
  • Writing Accuracy

For a research methods course, our group compared two ways of scoring grammatical accuracy in student writing: the General Accuracy Ratio (GAR) and the Error-Free Clause Ratio (EFCR). Using timed essays from 62 students at BYU's English Language Center and repeated-measures ANOVA, we tested how practical and valid each method was — finding that neither approach is universally better, and that the right choice depends on learners' proficiency and the goals of the assessment.

Literature Review

Motivation in Second Language Learning

  • L2 Motivation
  • Reading Motivation
  • Language Acquisition

Written for a Language Acquisition course, this literature overview examines how motivation shapes second language learning, drawing on Gardner's Socio-Educational Model and Dörnyei's Motivational Self System. It traces the distinct motivational challenges learners face at the beginner, intermediate, and advanced levels, and looks closely at reading motivation as one of the strongest predictors of long-term language learning success.

Research Proposal

fMRI Evidence of Vocabulary Knowledge in L2 Learners

  • Neurolinguistics
  • fMRI Methodology

Developed for a Neurolinguistics course, this proposal outlines a study investigating how the brain processes known versus unknown vocabulary in second language learners. Grounded in prior neuroimaging research on semantic and lexical processing, the design would test whether patterns of neural activation correlate with language proficiency and word familiarity, using a combined fMRI task and post-scan vocabulary test.
